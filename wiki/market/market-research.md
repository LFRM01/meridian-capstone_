# Market Research

Outside research (not from the client brief) on the specialty grocery
industry and the LA/Orange/Ventura market, to ground interview questions
in market context Dana didn't hand us.

## Specialty grocery industry trends

- Specialty grocers were "the fastest-growing category" in 2025, while
  traditional/regional supermarket operators face a "collapse of the
  middle" — losing share on both sides to discounters and specialty
  grocers at once. [8 grocery industry trends to watch in 2026 - Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/8-top-trends-grocery-industry-2026/807564/)
- FMI's annual food-retail survey found 75% of grocers now run fresh
  prepared-foodservice programs (82% of those are expanding grab-and-go
  space), and 72% report expanding locally-sourced offerings — the same
  two levers (prepared foods, local sourcing) Meridian's brief cites as
  its differentiation. [Food Retailers Pursuing Strategic Differentiation Strategies - The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2022/09/30/food-retailers-pursuing-strategic-differentiation-strategies/)
- The 15 largest chains still capture roughly half of all grocery visits,
  but specialty/independent grocers are carving out share through
  short, "mission-driven" trips and strength in perimeter categories
  (produce, plant-based, local/exclusive SKUs) rather than competing on
  full-basket, stock-up shopping. [Who's Winning Grocery Growth: Chains vs. Specialty Stores - FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/03/06/whos-winning-grocery-growth-chains-vs-specialty-stores/)
- Trader Joe's grew 3% year-over-year, 6 points ahead of the broader
  ~$912B U.S. grocery sector, with "double-digit year-over-year growth
  among Gen Z shoppers" — while traditional grocers like Publix and
  Safeway see declining traffic, especially among lower-income shoppers.
  Grocery-anchored real estate is following the same trend: transaction
  volume hit roughly $11B in 2025, up 42% year-over-year, as investors
  chase the centers specialty and value grocers anchor. [New grocery formats, store growth and investment signal sector in motion - ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/new-grocery-formats-store-growth-and-investment-signal-sector-in-motion)
- Grocery-anchored retail space is genuinely scarce: vacancy compressed
  to 3.5% (Q4 2024, a post-pandemic low) on "minimal new supply" — under
  100,000 sq ft of net deliveries for a second straight year, versus a
  2015-2019 average of 1.1M sq ft/year — and available space is down
  nearly half (~19M sq ft) from its post-recession peak. Grocery-anchored
  rent growth (3.1% YoY) now outpaces every other retail subtype. JLL's
  own conclusion: this scarcity "will impact the long-term growth plans
  of expanding grocers as they struggle to find desirable space in ideal
  locations" — directly relevant to a chain that grows by taking over
  exiting tenants' leases. Nationally, specialty grocers keep opening at
  pace despite this (Sprouts +35 stores/840K sq ft, Trader Joe's +34
  stores/450K sq ft in 2024) and are winning outsized foot-traffic growth
  (Trader Joe's visits up 24.7% since 2019 to 390M in 2024). [Grocery Report 2025: Key Market Insights - JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-perspectives/grocery-tracker)
- JLL's follow-up 2026 tracker frames this as a "barbell" market: food
  costs are up over 30% since early 2020, splitting shoppers into
  higher-income households trading up to premium/wellness spending and
  middle/lower-income households trading down to value formats and
  multi-store "bite-sized trips." The winners sit at either end of that
  barbell — value players (Aldi opened 180 stores in 2025 with another
  180 planned for 2026, 8.3% same-store visit growth) and fresh-format
  specialty grocers (Trader Joe's +10.4% foot traffic, Whole Foods
  +9.8%) — while traditional supermarkets are squeezed in the middle
  (Kroger's same-store visits essentially flat at 0.3%; traditional
  grocers' overall visit share slipped from 75.1% to 73.2%). The
  report's own framing: "the future belongs to properties anchored by
  the grocers capturing visit growth: value players... and fresh-format
  operators... not simply any grocery tenant." (This tracker also
  updates the real-estate figures with newer data: 4.0% grocery-anchored
  vacancy vs. 6.3% for non-anchored centers, and institutional investors
  now capturing 27% of acquisitions — the highest share in over a
  decade.) [Grocery Winners Are Built For Value, Fresh Format And Convenience - JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-ca/insights/market-perspectives/grocery-winners-are-built-for-value-fresh-format-and-convenience)

## LA / Orange / Ventura competitive landscape

- County-level market share: in LA County, Kroger 14%, Albertsons 11%,
  Target 9%; in Orange County, Albertsons 16%, Kroger 11%, Target 11%;
  in Ventura County, Albertsons 20%, Kroger 9%, Walmart 8% — the majors
  dominate but no single chain clears a third of any county. [This Grocery Chain Dominates In CA As Industry Shrinks: New Data - CA Patch](https://patch.com/california/across-ca/grocery-chain-dominates-ca-industry-shrinks-new-data)
- Specialty chains are actively expanding into Meridian's counties:
  Sprouts is adding in-store cafes at Huntington Beach, Glendale, La
  Verne, Diamond Bar, and West Covina locations — and, just outside
  Meridian's three-county footprint (San Bernardino County), Rancho
  Cucamonga — and Trader Joe's 2026 store-growth plan includes a new
  Anaheim Hills (Orange County) location. [Sprouts to open 20 in-store cafes across Southern California - Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/sprouts-farmers-market-klatch-coffee-shop-instore-restaurant-food-service-specialty-grocer/761166/), [Trader Joe's unveils plans for 8 more stores - Grocery Dive](https://www.grocerydive.com/news/trader-joes-store-growth-2026/816131/)
- Multicultural/international grocers (Super King Markets, H Mart,
  Mitsuwa Marketplace, Zion Market) are also strong competitors in the
  LA/Orange corridor, particularly in ethnically diverse neighborhoods
  near Irvine and Glassell Park. [The 22 Best (and Worst) Grocery Stores in Los Angeles - GroceryChop](https://grocerychop.com/blog/best-grocery-stores-los-angeles)
- Gelson's — an LA-area premium specialty chain and a direct comp for
  Meridian's positioning — is piloting small-format "ReCharge"
  micromarkets (5,400 and 3,000 sq. ft.) that pair premium convenience
  retail with EV charging. Separately, Brixton Capital paid $100M for
  the Quad at Whittier (314,593 sq. ft., 95.6% leased) — a
  grocery-anchored center inside LA County, Meridian's own footprint —
  showing real investor demand for the same class of property Meridian
  grows by taking over. [New grocery formats, store growth and investment signal sector in motion - ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/new-grocery-formats-store-growth-and-investment-signal-sector-in-motion)

## Chains exiting neighborhoods (Meridian's growth mechanism)

- Kroger is closing 60 "underperforming" stores nationwide as part of a
  "tactical consolidation," including a Food 4 Less at 19200 Soledad
  Canyon Road in Santa Clarita — inside LA County, Meridian's own
  footprint — which will be converted to a Ralphs rather than left
  vacant. [US grocery chain to close 60 'underperforming' stores - Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/us-grocery-chain-close-60-233237710.html)
- Grocery Outlet is closing 36 stores nationwide (9 in California),
  including locations in Azusa (LA County) and La Habra (Orange
  County) — both inside Meridian's three-county footprint — after
  determining they had "no viable path to sustained profitability,"
  despite 11% net sales growth companywide. [Grocery Chain Set To Close 9 CA Locations: See Where - LA Patch](https://patch.com/california/los-angeles/grocery-chain-set-close-6-socal-locations-see-where)
- These closures are attributed to rising labor costs, supply-chain
  pressure, and competition from discounters and e-commerce grocery —
  the same forces described in the industry-trends research above as
  squeezing "the middle" of the market, which is what turns over the
  leases a smaller specialty operator like Meridian can take. [91-year-old grocery chain announces closures for longtime locations - AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/91-old-grocery-chain-announces-133300000.html)
